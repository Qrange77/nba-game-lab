# Who helped their teammates more in 2015-16, Curry or LeBron?

# On-Court Impact Review

## Answer

**My position: a reviewed overall preference is unavailable. The supported dimension-level findings below remain valid; this does not establish one overall winner or a tie.**

## Dimension findings

- **Offense and era:** The indicators disagree; one player does not dominate all selected measures.
- **Playmaking and ball security:** The indicators disagree; one player does not dominate all selected measures.
- **Team on/off context:** Regular Season: Stephen Curry has the higher on-minus-off net rating; Playoffs: LeBron James has the higher on-minus-off net rating. This describes team association, not an isolated player effect.

This position compares observed contributions, not innate ability. Offensive judgement uses PPG and TS; league-relative efficiency is a separate cross-era check. Playmaking uses assists per minute and assists/turnovers, not a complete model of creation. Team on/off is association rather than causation.

**Scope:** 2015-16–2015-16 versus 2015-16–2015-16; Regular Season, Playoffs.

## Why we investigated further

- **Finding:** LeBron James has higher playoff PPG but lower TS. Check minutes, attempts and per-minute production before interpreting more points as broad improvement. [Evidence](https://stats.nba.com/stats/playercareerstats?PlayerID=2544&PerMode=Totals&LeagueID=00)
- **Follow-up: Completed.** Matched minutes, attempts and per-minute scoring are shown below. More PPG alone does not establish broader improvement.
- **What the follow-up changes:** LeBron James has higher playoff PPG but lower shooting efficiency. Minutes/game changed from 35.64 to 39.14, attempts/game from 18.63 to 19.86, and points/36 minutes from 25.51 to 24.18. This separates additional playing/shot opportunity from efficiency; higher PPG is not an across-the-board improvement. ## Coverage and limits

- Historical box-score evidence is not equivalent to modern tracking or lineup data. Missing historical coverage does not count against a player.
- Steals, blocks and rebounds describe recorded activity; matchup difficulty, positioning, deterrence and defensive schemes are not measured here.
- On/off comparisons are confounded by lineup, opponent and substitution choices. No causal team/winning impact is established.
- Small or unequal appearance samples and stale API responses remain limitations. No synthetic overall impact score is used.
