### 4. Methodological Stress Test: Does Net-Rating Association Alone Establish Individual Impact?

A common analytical trap is treating On/Off Net Rating differentials as pure, isolated measures of individual player skill. To test whether the net-rating association *alone* establishes individual impact, we must subject the metric to rigorous statistical scrutiny.

#### A. The Confounding Variable: Lineup Context and Rotation Patterns
On/Off statistics are inherently **lineup-dependent**. A player’s "Off-Court" data is not played in a vacuum; it is heavily influenced by who else is on the floor and the quality of the opponent's bench units.
*   **The Curry Test:** Curry shared the floor overwhelmingly with Golden State’s "Hamptons Five" core (Draymond Green, Klay Thompson, Andre Iguodala). When Curry sat, Steve Kerr frequently pulled multiple starters simultaneously to preserve rhythm, meaning Curry’s "Off-Court" minutes often coincided with heavy bench lineups (e.g., Shaun Livingston, Leandro Barbosa, Marreese Speights, Festus Ezeli). While still a formidable bench, the drop in talent from Curry to his replacement is compounded by the simultaneous subtraction of other elite starters.
*   **The LeBron Test:** Cleveland often deployed hockey-style or staggered rotations. LeBron frequently shared the floor with floor-spacers (Channing Frye, J.R. Smith) or saw minutes alongside secondary units where Kyrie Irving or Kevin Love ran the show. 

#### B. The Multi-Collinearity Problem
Net rating is a composite of Offensive Rating and Defensive Rating. Neither component is entirely clean:
*   **Defensive On/Off Noise:** Defensive rating is notoriously noisy over a single regular season. A team can post a phenomenal defensive rating with a specific player on the floor primarily because of a low opponent shooting percentage on uncontested 3-pointers—a variable governed more by variance than a single perimeter player's individual defensive footprint. 
*   **The Gravity Effect:** Curry’s historic on/off offense is driven heavily by *gravitational pull* (drawing double-teams 35 feet from the basket). However, tracking how that gravity manifests for his teammates requires spatial data, not just raw net-rating association.

#### C. Conclusion of the Test
**No, net-rating association alone does not fully establish individual impact.** 

While On/Off metrics are powerful indicators of team success correlated with a player's presence, they conflate:
1.  **Individual skill and gravity** (Curry's shooting, LeBron's passing/scoring).
2.  **Coach rotation strategies** (staggering vs. mass substitutions).
3.  **Teammate synergy and roster construction** (the quality of the supporting cast sharing the court).
